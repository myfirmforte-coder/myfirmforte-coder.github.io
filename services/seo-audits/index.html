THE TROUBLE WITH AUDITS

Most SEO audits are noise disguised as insight.

If you’ve ever received a 60-page audit that left you more confused than when you started, here’s why.

🤖
Automated reports find symptoms, not causes
Screaming Frog and Semrush dump thousands of 'issues' into a PDF. But Google doesn't care about 95% of them. Without a human to separate real bottlenecks from background noise, you get overwhelmed, not informed.
📄
Generic audits miss what's actually wrong
Every business has a unique ranking profile. A templated audit that runs the same checks on a law firm, a SaaS, and an e-commerce store will miss the industry-specific issues that actually matter for each.
💤
No action plan means no action
A list of 300 issues with no prioritization, no effort estimates, and no implementation guidance gets filed away and forgotten. A great audit tells you what to do first, second, and third — with the expected impact of each.

Ten layers of analysis, one clear action plan.

Every Rank Ready audit covers the full stack — from technical foundations to AI visibility. The deliverable is focused on what to fix, not just what’s broken.

Prioritized Action Plan
Every finding scored by impact and effort. A clear 30/60/90-day roadmap with what to do first, what to delegate, and what to ignore entirely.

WHY AUDITS MATTER

Without diagnosis, you’re guessing. And guessing is expensive.

Most ranking problems come down to 2-3 root causes

After auditing hundreds of sites, a pattern emerges: 80% of ranking problems stem from 2-3 root causes, not 200 small issues. Identifying those root causes is the job of a real audit. Finding them requires pattern recognition that comes from experience — not a software tool. Once you know what's actually broken, fixing it is usually the easy part.

Opportunity cost is the real cost of guessing

Every month you spend fixing the wrong issues is a month your competitors are pulling ahead. A proper audit compresses that cycle — instead of trying ten things to see what works, you fix the two things that matter. Most audit clients recoup the audit cost within 60-90 days through accelerated ranking gains alone.

Pre-migration audits prevent catastrophic losses

Site redesigns, replatforms, and domain migrations are the single most common cause of catastrophic traffic drops. A proper pre-migration audit (URL mapping, redirect strategy, schema preservation, content parity) can prevent 90% of those losses. Running an audit before a migration costs a fraction of recovering after one.

Hiring decisions benefit from a neutral third-party view

If you're evaluating whether to hire an agency, retain your current one, or bring SEO in-house, an independent audit gives you neutral ground truth. You'll see what's working, what's not, and what a good agency would prioritize — which makes every vendor conversation sharper.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Audit questions, answered.

Everything you’d want to know before requesting an audit.

Is the first audit really free?

Yes — genuinely free, no strings. We offer a free SEO + GEO audit because it’s the most honest way to start a conversation. You get a real, senior-led audit covering your top priorities. If you want to hire us afterward, great. If you want to take the recommendations and implement them with a different team, also great. We’ve had plenty of audit recipients come back 6-12 months later to hire us — the long game works better than hard selling.

What's the difference between the free audit and a paid one?

The free audit is a focused, top-priorities diagnostic — usually 5-8 pages covering the 3-5 biggest issues and a prioritized action plan. It takes us about 4-6 hours of work. The paid comprehensive audit is the full 10-layer deep dive documented on this page: technical, content, semantic, backlinks, local, GEO, competitive benchmarking, and a detailed 30/60/90-day roadmap. It’s 15-25 pages and takes a senior strategist 12-20 hours.

How long does a paid audit take?

5-7 business days from kickoff call to delivery for most sites. Very large sites (1000+ pages, complex multi-domain setups, enterprise e-commerce) may take 10-14 days. We don’t stretch timelines artificially — every day we delay is a day you can’t act on the recommendations.

What's the deliverable format?

A structured audit document (PDF + Notion/Google Doc if preferred) with findings organized by priority, not just by category. Each priority includes: the issue, why it matters, the specific fix, effort estimate, expected impact, and links to related tools or resources. Plus a recorded walkthrough call where we present findings and answer questions.

Do I have to hire Rank Ready after the audit?

Absolutely not. The audit is a standalone deliverable. You own the recommendations and can implement them with your in-house team, a different agency, or keep them in your back pocket. Roughly 40% of audit clients hire us afterward for implementation, 40% implement with their existing team, and 20% wait and come back later. All three paths are fine with us.

What platforms and site types can you audit?

We audit sites on WordPress, Divi, Elementor, Shopify, WooCommerce, Webflow, Wix, Squarespace, Magento, BigCommerce, HubSpot CMS, custom Next.js/Nuxt builds, and everything in between. The underlying SEO principles apply across platforms — the implementation guidance adapts. We’ve never had a platform we couldn’t audit.

How technical does the audit get?

As technical as needed, but always written in plain English. We cover log file analysis, crawl budget optimization, JavaScript rendering issues, server response analysis, and schema validation when those are relevant. But every finding is explained so a marketing lead (not just a developer) can understand what’s happening and why it matters.

Do you audit for both SEO and GEO?

Yes — every audit includes both. Given how overlapping modern SEO and GEO are, it makes no sense to audit one without the other. You’ll see your traditional ranking readiness alongside your AI citation readiness, and how the two compound. This is one of the things that makes Rank Ready audits distinctive — most agencies still aren’t auditing for GEO at all.
